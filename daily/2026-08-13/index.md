  - id: "2026-08-13-1"
    question: "What 14th-century emperor of the Mali Empire is generally regarded as the wealthiest person in recorded history, thanks to his realm's near-monopoly on West African gold and salt trade routes?"
    answer: "Mansa Musa"
    topic: famous-people
    explanation: >-
      Mansa Musa ruled the Mali Empire in West Africa from roughly 1312 to
      1337, presiding over a state that stretched across much of the Sahel
      and controlled its most valuable trade routes. Mali is thought to
      have supplied close to half the world's gold at the time, and that
      near-monopoly made Musa so wealthy that modern rankings still list
      him as arguably the richest individual in history, with a fortune
      too large to meaningfully convert into today's dollars. He's best
      known outside Mali for a single event: a 1324 pilgrimage to Mecca so
      extravagant that it left a trail of records, legends, and even a
      European map still shaping how historians reconstruct medieval West
      African history today.
  - id: "2026-08-13-2"
    question: "Mansa Musa's lavish gold spending during a stopover devalued the currency of what city for over a decade afterward?"
    answer: "Cairo"
    topic: famous-people
    explanation: >-
      Passing through Cairo on his way to Mecca, Mansa Musa gave away and
      spent so much gold that he flooded the local market with it,
      crashing its value. Gold prices in the city reportedly stayed
      depressed for roughly twelve years afterward, a striking example of
      one man's spending measurably reshaping a regional economy centuries
      before anyone had a word for inflation.
  - id: "2026-08-13-3"
    question: "What Andalusian poet and architect did Mansa Musa recruit in Mecca to help design Timbuktu's Djinguereber Mosque?"
    answer: "Al-Sahili"
    topic: famous-people
    explanation: >-
      On his way home from Mecca, Mansa Musa met Abu Ishaq al-Sahili, a
      poet and builder from Granada, and was so taken with his talents
      that he invited him back to Mali. Al-Sahili is credited with
      influencing the design of Timbuktu's Djinguereber Mosque and a royal
      audience chamber, helping seed the distinctive mudbrick architectural
      style that would come to define the city.
  - id: "2026-08-13-4"
    question: "What 1375 map by Majorcan cartographers first depicted Mansa Musa enthroned with a gold nugget, putting Mali on Europe's radar?"
    answer: "Catalan Atlas"
    topic: famous-people
    explanation: >-
      Mansa Musa's reputation crossed the Mediterranean well before most
      Europeans had any real knowledge of West Africa. The Catalan Atlas
      of 1375, made by Majorcan cartographer Abraham Cresques, shows him
      seated on a throne in the middle of the continent, crowned and
      holding a golden nugget the size of an apple. It's one of the
      earliest depictions of a sub-Saharan African ruler in European
      cartography.
  - id: "2026-08-13-5"
    question: "Roughly how many tons of gold did Mansa Musa reportedly carry with him on his hajj to Mecca?"
    answer: "18"
    topic: famous-people
    explanation: >-
      Estimates of Mansa Musa's hajj gold vary, but historians commonly
      cite a figure around 18 tons carried across the journey, drawn from
      the salt and gold trade routes his empire controlled. Mali is
      thought to have supplied close to half the world's gold at the time,
      which is part of why modern rankings still list Mansa Musa as
      arguably the wealthiest individual in recorded history.

  - id: "2026-08-22-1"
    question: "What single term, coined in 1982 for an infectious agent made purely of misfolded protein with no DNA or RNA of its own, describes a particle that forces normal copies of that same protein to misfold in turn?"
    answer: "Prion"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      Prions are misfolded versions of a protein called PrP that naturally
      sits on the surface of nerve cells. When a misfolded PrP molecule
      touches a normal one, it acts like a template, flipping the healthy
      copy into the same abnormal shape and setting off a chain reaction.
      Neurologist Stanley Prusiner proposed the idea in 1982 after studying
      scrapie, a sheep disease, and it was so radical — a pathogen with no
      genetic material — that it took years, and a Nobel Prize in 1997,
      before most scientists accepted it. Human prion diseases include
      Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease and kuru; the same mechanism causes scrapie
      in sheep, chronic wasting disease in deer, and bovine spongiform
      encephalopathy, better known as mad cow disease, in cattle.
  - id: "2026-08-22-2"
    question: "What caustic chemical, alongside prolonged high-temperature autoclaving well beyond routine hospital settings, is one of the few treatments known to reliably destroy prions clinging to surgical instruments?"
    answer: "Sodium hydroxide"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      Prions have no genome to damage and are far more heat- and
      chemical-resistant than viruses or bacteria, so the sterilization
      routines that kill ordinary pathogens often leave infectious prion
      protein intact. Standard hospital autoclaving, boiling, alcohol, and
      formaldehyde all fail to fully neutralize them. Guidelines for
      instruments exposed to suspected prion disease call for soaking them
      in concentrated sodium hydroxide, or lye, combined with extended
      autoclaving at higher-than-normal temperatures, or simply incinerating
      the instruments outright. That resistance is also why a handful of
      Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease cases have been traced to contaminated
      neurosurgical tools and human-derived growth hormone decades ago.
  - id: "2026-08-22-3"
    question: "What American virologist won the 1976 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for proving that kuru was a slow, transmissible infection, two decades before the 'prion' concept explained what was actually spreading it?"
    answer: "Carleton Gajdusek"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      Gajdusek traveled to Papua New Guinea in the late 1950s to investigate
      kuru, a fatal illness causing tremors and loss of coordination. By
      injecting chimpanzees with tissue from kuru victims' brains and
      watching them develop the same symptoms years later, he demonstrated
      the disease could be transmitted like an infection, even though no
      virus or bacterium could ever be found in it. That work won him a
      Nobel Prize before anyone knew the infectious agent was a misfolded
      protein rather than a microbe, and it directly inspired Prusiner's
      later prion hypothesis.
  - id: "2026-08-22-4"
    question: "What Papua New Guinea highlands people experienced a devastating 20th-century kuru epidemic, tied to a mortuary rite in which relatives ate the tissue of their deceased kin?"
    answer: "Fore"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      At its peak in the 1950s, kuru killed a significant share of the Fore
      population each year, and because the mortuary rite was carried out
      mainly by women and children, they bore the overwhelming majority of
      cases — in some villages, adult women came to outnumber adult men by
      as much as eight to one. The practice ended after Australian colonial
      authorities banned it in the late 1950s, and kuru cases declined for
      decades afterward, though a few still appeared as late as the 2000s
      because the disease's incubation period can stretch beyond 50 years.
      Studying the Fore also led researchers to a natural genetic variant
      that appears to protect against prion disease.
  - id: "2026-08-22-5"
    question: "What nickname, evoking a horror-movie label, has become the popular name for chronic wasting disease as it spreads through deer, elk, and moose herds across North America?"
    answer: "Zombie deer disease"
    topic: science
    explanation: >-
      Chronic wasting disease is a prion illness that leaves infected
      cervids drooling, stumbling, drastically underweight, and unafraid of
      humans, which is how the nickname caught on in media coverage. It has
      now been confirmed in wild or captive herds across dozens of U.S.
      states and several countries, and unlike most prion diseases, it
      spreads efficiently between animals through saliva, urine, and feces,
      and can persist in soil for years. No human cases have been
      confirmed, but health agencies monitor it closely because other prion
      diseases have crossed from animals to people before, as bovine
      spongiform encephalopathy did in the 1990s.

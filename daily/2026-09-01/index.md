topic: "The Dancing Plague of 1518"
facts:
  - id: "2026-09-01-1"
    question: "What is the name for the outbreak that began in Strasbourg in July 1518, when a woman known as Frau Troffea started dancing alone in the street and couldn't stop, and by the following month roughly 400 townspeople had joined her in uncontrollable dancing?"
    answer: "Dancing plague"
    topic: history
    explanation: >-
      In July 1518, in the Alsatian city of Strasbourg, a woman named Frau
      Troffea began dancing in the street with no music and no apparent
      reason, and kept going for days without stopping to rest. Within a
      week roughly 34 others had joined her, and within a month the crowd of
      afflicted dancers had swelled to around 400. The episode, now known
      as the dancing plague of 1518, is the best documented of more than ten
      similar dancing manias recorded along the Rhine and Moselle rivers
      since 1374, and it set off a civic response that ended up making
      things worse rather than better.
  - id: "2026-09-01-2"
    question: "Believing that Strasbourg's stricken dancers of 1518 needed to sweat their affliction out rather than be restrained, what type of buildings did the city's authorities clear out and turn into dance floors, complete with hired musicians and a wooden stage?"
    answer: "Guild halls"
    topic: history
    explanation: >-
      Strasbourg's town council, guided by physicians who diagnosed the
      dancers with dangerously overheated "hot blood," decided the cure was
      to let the mania run its course through continuous movement rather
      than suppress it. Officials had grain and carpenters' guild halls
      cleared out, built an open-air stage, and paid musicians to play
      pipes, drums, and horns to keep the afflicted moving. The strategy
      backfired: instead of burning itself out, the spectacle is thought to
      have drawn in more onlookers and exhausted the dancers further, with
      some reportedly collapsing or dying from the strain.
  - id: "2026-09-01-3"
    question: "Which saint, blamed in German folklore for cursing people with uncontrollable dancing, did Strasbourg officials eventually send the worst-afflicted 1518 dancers on a three-day wagon journey to a shrine of, complete with red shoes and holy water?"
    answer: "Saint Vitus"
    topic: history
    explanation: >-
      When the guild-hall dance floors failed to end the mania, Strasbourg's
      authorities shifted from a medical explanation to a religious one,
      concluding the dancers were suffering under a curse from Saint Vitus,
      a third-century martyr tied in regional folklore to fits of compulsive
      dancing. The worst-affected dancers were loaded onto wagons for the
      three-day journey to a shrine of his in the town of Saverne, where
      they were fitted with red shoes marked with holy water and crosses of
      consecrated oil and led around a wooden statue of the saint. The
      mania faded within a couple of months of its start, and word of the
      pilgrimage's apparent success drew more sufferers to make the same
      trip.
  - id: "2026-09-01-4"
    question: "What toxic mold that grows on damp rye, capable of causing spasms and hallucinations, has been proposed as an explanation for the 1518 Strasbourg dancing mania but is now largely dismissed by historians?"
    answer: "Ergot"
    topic: history
    explanation: >-
      One long-standing theory blamed ergot poisoning, since the fungus
      thrives on rye in wet conditions and can trigger convulsions and
      hallucinations when eaten in contaminated bread. Most historians now
      reject this explanation, though, because ergot poisoning constricts
      blood vessels and causes jerky, involuntary spasms that would have
      made sustained, coordinated dancing for days at a stretch physically
      implausible. It also fails to explain why the affliction spread from
      person to person the way it did, rather than striking everyone who
      ate from the same contaminated grain supply.
  - id: "2026-09-01-5"
    question: "What term do historians such as John Waller use for the stress-triggered group behavior they believe explains the 1518 Strasbourg dancing mania, pointing to the famine and disease bearing down on the city's residents that year?"
    answer: "Mass psychogenic illness"
    topic: history
    explanation: >-
      Historian John Waller's widely cited theory holds that the dancing
      plague was a form of mass psychogenic illness, a stress-induced
      collective breakdown that can spread through a population under
      extreme psychological strain. In 1518, Strasbourg's residents were
      reeling from famine and diseases including smallpox and syphilis,
      conditions Waller argues primed people who already believed in Saint
      Vitus's curse to fall into an involuntary, trance-like dancing state
      after seeing others do the same. The theory helps explain why the
      mania spread through observation and belief rather than through any
      physical contagion.
